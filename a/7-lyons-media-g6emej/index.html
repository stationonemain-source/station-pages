01One thing to fix before anything else

We want to be straight with you about this, because it matters — and equally straight that it's common, fixable in an afternoon, and not something you could have caught.

Fix firstSomeone has planted hidden spam links on four of your pages

At some point the site was compromised, and a handful of pages now carry hidden links to other people's websites — the kind that advertise pharmaceuticals, gambling and adult content. They're written into the code in ways designed to be read by search engines and missed by people, and on one page a couple of them are actually visible if you read far enough down.

Why you'd never have seen it. The four affected pages are ones nothing on your site links to — the privacy policy, a leftover "sample page" WordPress creates by itself, and two early pages from when the site was built. There is no button or menu that leads to them; the only way to arrive is to type the address. That's exactly why they were chosen. The pages you and your clients actually use — the homepage, About, Our Work, Consulting, Contact — are untouched.

What it is and isn't. This is aimed at search engines, not at your visitors. We saw no sign of anything targeting the people who come to your site or their information. What it does affect is how Google and any client who runs a routine check on your web address perceive the domain — which, for a company that pitches networks and brands, is the part worth moving quickly on.

What we'll do. Remove every trace, change the passwords, and close the door it came through (the site currently gives away its login username to anyone who asks, which makes it easier to break into than it should be). Then keep an eye on it so it stays clean. We'll walk you through exactly where it all is tomorrow — it's easier to see than to describe.

02Leads are slipping through

We clicked every button on the site through to where it ends up, rather than assuming it works. Most of what we found here is quick to fix — and one of them we'd fix tonight if it were ours.

Fix tonight"Book a Consultation" leads to a locked form

The main button on the Consulting page points to a form that's been switched to private. Anyone who clicks it is sent off your site to this message:

"Sorry, you can't access this typeform until its creator says so."

That's the button that sells your consulting, and it hasn't been reaching anyone. The fix is one setting in Typeform — about two minutes — and it's the single most valuable change in this whole document.

Worth fixingThe contact form asks for a name and an email, and nothing else

No message box, no phone number, no way to say what the project is. Every enquiry arrives as a mystery, so each one costs a round of emails before you can tell whether it's a network series or a wedding video.

Worth fixingNo phone number, and the email address is a personal Gmail

There's no phone number anywhere on the site, and the published address is a personal Gmail account. Neither is a dealbreaker with people who already know you — but for a producer at a brand or a network doing their due diligence, an @7lyonsmedia.com address and a number to call quietly remove a hesitation you'd never hear about.

Blind spotThe site has no analytics at all

No Google Analytics, no visitor tracking of any kind. That's why none of the above ever surfaced — there's currently no way to know how many people arrived, clicked that consultation button, and left. The new site will fix this on day one, and it's the part that makes everything else measurable.

Working"Get Started" and your Calendly link both work

The full-service interest form loads correctly, and the free 15-minute call link goes where it should. We'll double-check the calendar's availability with you tomorrow rather than guess at it.

03Being found

Free to fixThere's no Google Business Profile

Google's local listings have no record of 7 Lyons Media — no map pin, no reviews, no "call" button. Searching the company name in Google Maps brings up other people, including a Lyons Media Production in Michigan with 67 reviews. Meanwhile, within 30 km of downtown LA:

Twenty companies in that radius hold 543 reviews between them, and not one of them has your credits. A profile is free and takes about twenty minutes; the reviews follow from the clients you already have.

The site doesn't link to any of your social accounts

Your LinkedIn, Facebook and X accounts all exist and are active, but the website doesn't point to any of them — the only outbound links on the whole site are two Vimeo embeds. For a company that consults on social media, the website should be the most connected thing you own. Easy to fix in the new build.

04The site itself

Your reel is the best thing on the site, and it's the thing that loads slowest

The homepage carries about 198 MB of video — three files, two of them set to autoplay, one of them a 98 MB QuickTime file. On our test the page took 46 seconds to finish loading. On a phone on mobile data, a lot of people will leave before the reel has started. The fix is squarely in your team's wheelhouse: the same footage, re-encoded for the web, comes in around 5 MB and plays instantly.

A little housekeeping

A few pages that were never meant to be public are live and being sent to Google: the default WordPress "Sample Page", two early test versions of the homepage (one still shows placeholder text and the old company name), and a second, older Consulting page that competes with the real one. None of it is visible from the menu; all of it should go.

06How we checked

  • Read every page in the site's sitemap (12 pages) from the outside, on desktop and on a simulated phone.
  • Clicked every button and link through to its final destination, including the two forms and the calendar.
  • Measured load time and file sizes in a real browser.
  • Checked Google's local business index for the company, and for competitors within 30 km.
  • Checked for analytics and tracking at the network level.
  • Verified the hidden links by reading the page code and by confirming how the browser actually renders them.

Two things we didn't establish and won't claim: whether the Calendly calendar currently has open slots (we'll check with you), and whether Google has already reacted to the spam links (that needs your Search Console, which we'll set up together).
